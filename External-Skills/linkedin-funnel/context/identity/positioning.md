# Positioning

## Audience

Companies, conferences, corporate leadership teams, and professional and trade organizations. Events focused on HR or leadership are an especially strong fit, since her message connects directly to keeping good employees. Broader than the uniform/apparel niche her first major booking (NAUMD) came from.

## Problem

Most companies claim values but can't show where those values actually show up, in hiring, in who gets trained, in how a client relationship is handled, in what happens in the community, not just what gets posted about it. That gap is expensive: lost retention, lost trust, lost differentiation, not just a moral inconsistency.

## Point of view

Not an ethical question first, a financial one first. Turnover, lost trust, lost differentiation, with the ethical payoff as the result of fixing it, not the pitch itself. Her challenge to every audience: "If these are truly our values, where can people actually see them in our business?"

## Promise

Move an organization from having values to operating by them, through a keynote and a hands-on workshop, tools they can keep using after she leaves. Not ongoing consulting, not diagnosis-and-roadmap, education and activation, the organization owns implementation.

## Evidence

Practitioner, not theorist: she put her own company through a real, actual B Corp certification (third-party audit, scored across governance, workers, community, environment, and customers, not self-reported), before ever telling anyone else to do the same. Score of 115.0 vs. a 50.9 median for ordinary businesses, more than double. Also built the nation's first DOL-registered apprenticeship in industrial sewing (independently verified), turning a disappearing trade into a credentialed career path.

## What to stand against

The say-do gap in any form: greenwashing, a waste-driven business marketed as sustainable, hustle-culture messaging that glamorizes success at the cost of other people or their own stated values, or any claim that can't be backed up with proof. Fast fashion in any form, a direct contradiction of her TEDx thesis. Being flattened into "just a stylist," which undercuts her credibility as a serious business voice.

## Relationship to the company

Founder and CEO of Tad More Tailoring and Alterations (Rockford, IL). The company itself is her primary proof point, not a separate case study, she's the example, not someone citing one. Her separate personal-brand/styling site (Power Look) does not become a second keynote topic, it has a narrow, low-frequency place in the content plan only.
