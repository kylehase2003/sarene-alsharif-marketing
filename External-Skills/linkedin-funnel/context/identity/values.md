# Values

| Value | What it means in practice | Decision that demonstrates it | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repair over replace | "Do a tad more," fix what exists instead of discarding it, applied to clothes, people, and community, not just the environment. | The entire Tad More business model, plus B2B work rebuilding uniforms (e.g., Chick-fil-A weatherproof drive-thru uniforms) instead of quietly replacing them. | Master Reference |
| Investing in people as strategy, not charity | Training and second-chance opportunity treated as a business decision, not a donation. | The DOL-registered apprenticeship program in industrial sewing, built with The Workforce Connection, real cost, real credential, not a marketing gesture. | Master Reference |
| Second chances, for people and things | The same instinct that repairs a garment applies to who gets hired and trained. | Turning a disappearing trade into a credentialed career path via the apprenticeship program. | Master Reference |
| Staffing around real strengths, not one mold | Not everyone has to be a natural teacher to be valuable; training and roles get built around what a person is actually good at. | Maen (lead tailor, not a natural teacher) versus Noor and Adriana, who are, and who took on the training role instead. | Master Reference |
| Leading by example first | Growth and hard work start with her, before she asks it of her team or an audience. | Confirmed directly by her: "I expect the owner to work harder than everyone." | Master Reference, edit note 8, confirmed by her directly |
| Proof over claims | Values only count if they change a real decision, not just a statement. | Put her own company through an actual third-party B Corp audit (scored across governance, workers, community, environment, customers) rather than just claiming values on a mission page. | Master Reference |
| Real community investment, not a photo-op | Ongoing involvement, not a one-time performative gesture. | Restyle the Runway (annual fundraiser fashion show with Women's Space and Rockford University), Green Threads (fashion show from Tad More's fabric scraps). | Master Reference |
