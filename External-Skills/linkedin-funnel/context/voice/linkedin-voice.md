## Vocabulary and rhythm

Sourced from spoken delivery (TEDx talk, Restyle the Runway talk), not written LinkedIn samples, treat as a starting approximation, not a substitute for real written samples:
- Serious data delivered with dry, self-deprecating humor on a stage-style talk; warmer and more instructional in a community-talk setting.
- Every statistic paired with a tangible, human-scale comparison, never a bare number alone (e.g., 7,500 liters of water per pair of jeans framed as seven years of drinking water).
- Every claim gets a named, credible source, never a vague "studies show."
- One sustained metaphor carries a whole section of advice, rather than a disconnected list of tips.

## Point of view

Financial-first, not moral-first: "does a company's stated values actually show up in practice, or is it just marketing, and what is that gap costing them." Practitioner, not theorist, she demonstrates the point on herself before asking anyone else to try it. Confident and declarative, not tentative, avoid framing her own realizations as naive discovery ("I thought X, turns out Y"), state them as established authority instead.

## Hooks and endings

Closes on a single, quotable line, or a short, concrete action list, a confirmed structural pattern across both known talks. In Q&A, admits plainly when something is outside what she knows, rather than faking authority.

## Edits and rejected drafts

Real corrections made during actual script-writing sessions for this project, worth preserving as negative examples:
- A "here's what I learned in 30 seconds" script was first drafted with an "I thought X, turns out Y" structure on every line. Rejected: reads as inexperienced. Rewritten as confident, declarative statements instead.
- The same script was then rewritten with every line following an identical "it's not X, it's Y" rhetorical pattern. Rejected: mechanical, no longer sounds like a person talking. Fixed by varying sentence shape line to line.
- A script was flagged for lacking a concrete personal proof point. Correction from the user: not every script needs to close the loop with a specific example, her overall credibility is already established by her bio and the wider content mix, individual framework/opinion pieces can stand alone. See project memory `feedback_proof_lives_in_whole_body` for the full reasoning.
- When asked to make a script sound more emotionally driven around a hardship, specific hardship beats (a moment of doubt, a specific setback, a real cost) must not be invented, we don't have that material from her. The fix was writing toward the general, true feeling of the process (exposure, uncertainty, no hiding behind good intentions) without fabricating a specific event.

## Rules supported by the examples

- No em dashes, house style for this entire project, not specific to LinkedIn.
- Financial framing before ethical framing, every time.
- Confident/declarative delivery, not "I thought/I realized" naive-discovery framing.
- Vary sentence structure line to line in short-form scripts, don't repeat the same rhetorical device across every beat.
- Real proof points only, never invent a specific event, cost, or moment of doubt she hasn't actually told us about.
