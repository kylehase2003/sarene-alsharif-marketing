# Branding Methodology, Reference Guide

A working reference distilled from the branding course script, to be applied when building Sereen's strategy. Each section is a tool: a principle plus the questions used to apply it.

---

## 1. Core Definitions

- **Branding** = a pairing of things.
- **Good branding** = an *intentional* pairing of *relevant* things, done *consistently*.
- **Brand** = the byproduct, what happens once the audience starts making that association on their own, without being told to.

Purpose of this definition: make branding an operational, repeatable action, not an abstract quality.

---

## 2. The Brand Journey Framework

Worked backward from the end goal. Four questions, in order:

1. What is the desired outcome?
2. What do I have to be known for, in order for that to happen?
3. What do I have to *do* in order to be known for that? (brand is action, not just messaging)
4. What do I have to *learn* in order to do that? (this defines what to work on starting now)

Rule: without a defined outcome, every decision downstream is directionless.

---

## 3. Defining Associations

**What to be associated with:**
- What do I want the audience to think of when they hear this name?
- Every piece of content should reinforce that specific association.

**What NOT to be associated with** (treated as the more important, more commonly ignored half):
- Intentional consistency isn't automatically good, you can be intentionally consistent with the *wrong* things (e.g., repeated association with untrustworthy people/platforms) and the audience will still draw that link, regardless of intent.
- Rule of thumb: if an opportunity gives a bad gut feeling, the exposure isn't worth it, reach/awareness is not a fair trade for the wrong association.

---

## 4. Positioning, Finding the Gap

Goal is not to blend in. Find what's missing in the space and own it.

Two questions:
1. What are others already saying in this space that I disagree with?
2. How can I bring a fresh angle to something that's been taught the same way for years?

Differentiation can come from information, philosophy/stance, or personality, any of these can reach an audience the existing voices in the space aren't reaching.

---

## 5. Story as Differentiator

The story is the one asset nobody else can copy.

Exercise: list out:
- Pivotal moments
- Challenges overcome
- Career turning points
- Unique experiences that shaped perspective

Rule: specific stories persuade; generic trait-claims don't. "I'm hardworking" convinces no one, the specific story that proves it does.

---

## 6. Pre-Handling Skepticism Inside the Content

The audience is silently objecting the whole time ("does this actually work for someone like me?").

Method: mine existing comments/feedback for recurring objections and skepticism, then address them directly inside future content, the same way a sales script pre-handles objections before a prospect gets on a call. This builds trust and removes friction between the audience and taking action.

---

## 7. Humanizing the Content ("Don't Be a Robot")

- Bring personality, not monotone information delivery.
- Share losses and failures, not just wins, an unbroken highlight reel reads as fake and increases skepticism rather than reducing it. Failures are often more relatable and more instructive than wins.

---

## 8. Listening to the Audience, With a Guardrail

- Ignore trolling; don't get high on praise either (if you ignore the praise, the hate can't touch you).
- Genuinely mine feedback for what people already value and double down on it.
- **Guardrail:** use feedback to refine *how* the belief is presented, never to change *what* is actually believed just to please people. Catering the message to the audience is what makes something read as inauthentic.

---

## 9. The Brand Story Framework (Catalyst / Core Truth / Proof)

**1. Catalyst, why the brand exists.**
Something needed to change (not always a struggle, can be a gap or opportunity).
- What needs to change?
- What do I see that others don't?
- Why do I feel compelled to act on it?

**2. Core truth, what makes this genuinely different.**
A real conviction, held and stated publicly (distinct from manufactured controversy).
- What do I believe that others don't?
- What about my personality stands out?
- Why would the audience actually care, what value does this bring them?

**3. Proof, ongoing demonstrated credibility, not a single past win.**
Brand is what's repeatedly done and shown, not what's claimed.
- Does every piece of content reinforce the intended association?
- What case studies/examples establish credibility?
- What's the first thing someone thinks of when they hear this name?

---

## 10. Topic Selection

**Start narrow.** Early trust comes from depth in one specific thing, not generalist range. A person who talks about everything is trusted with nothing; a person obsessed with one specific thing reads as a credible expert in it.

**When to expand:**
- Only speak on what's been genuinely mastered, don't add a topic before reaching real competence in it.
- Expand once there's consistent positive engagement on the current topic.
- Expand into *adjacent* topics only, never an unrelated leap. Credibility in the new topic should be earned by demonstrated results in the current one.
- Watch for organic audience requests as a signal of what to expand into.
- "Expand in layers, not leaps", every new topic should feel like a natural next step to the existing audience.
- Periodically revisit the origin/roots as a nostalgia play, even while expanding, this doesn't mean abandoning where it started.

**Guardrail:** treat all of the above as guidelines, not rigid rules. Rigid "content pillars" that block genuinely interesting, humanizing content are counterproductive.

**Final filter, three questions for picking any given topic:**
1. What am I genuinely passionate about discussing? (conviction reads through and drives audience trust/action)
2. What will resonate most with the audience right now? (their actual current knowledge gap, not a generic topic)
3. **(Most important)** What will actually cause the audience to change their behavior, not just passively consume?

---

## 11. The 80/20 Rule + Interest Stacking

- **80%** of content = the one core thing to be known for, tied directly to the actual offer/revenue driver.
- **20%** = personal interests that round out the person behind the brand ("interest stacking").

Why the 20% matters: more genuine points of interest shared = more "at bats" for an audience member to personally connect ("I also like that thing"). This is what turns a single-topic follower into a superfan with multiple points of connection, not just people who admire the expertise, but people who feel a personal bond.

---

## Structural note

In the source material, nearly every framework above is demonstrated live using the speaker's own content choices, failures, and personal interests, the "proof" pillar of the framework is being executed in real time as it's taught. Worth keeping as a model: the strongest version of any of these principles isn't just stated, it's shown.
