## The one rule that matters most: topic first, evidence second

Never start from "I want to mention the B Corp score." Start from a topic (below), then attach whichever real piece of evidence actually supports it. This keeps content sustainable instead of repeating the same three achievements on a loop.

## Her voice, platform-independent rules

- Confident and declarative, not tentative. Never frame her own realizations as naive discovery ("I thought X, turns out Y," "I used to think... then I realized"). State things as established authority.
- Financial-first, not moral-first: the question is what a values gap actually costs a company, not a pure ethical appeal.
- Practitioner, not theorist. She demonstrates the point on herself before asking anyone else to try it.
- Not every post needs a personal proof point. A framework or opinion piece can stand alone.
- Don't invent a specific hardship, moment of doubt, or cost that isn't in this document. If a post needs emotional weight, write toward the general true feeling without fabricating a specific event.
- No "it's not X, it's Y" repeated on every line. Vary sentence shape.

## Instagram's own format rules (2026)

**The first 125 characters are the entire job.** Instagram hides everything after that behind "more." If line 1 doesn't earn the tap, nothing else gets read. Front-load the payoff, the tension, or a specific number.

**One idea per caption.** A caption arguing three points reads like a blog post. Pick the one point that earns the save.

**Length:** cap at 2,200 characters. Most strong captions land between 150 and 1,500. A carousel caption can be short since the slides carry the payload; a single-image caption leans harder on the words.

**Line breaks are punctuation.** White space controls pacing and keeps it skimmable. A wall of text gets scrolled past.

**Specific numbers beat adjectives.** "Saved 11 hours a week" beats "saved tons of time." Use odd, non-rounded figures where the fact allows it, they read as real measurement.

**Emoji:** 0 to 3 per caption, placed with intent, never sprinkled. Only in warmer or celebratory posts, same rule as everywhere else in this project.

**Don't hard-sell.** Teach or tell a story, then point to the next step once, max.

**End on a landing, not a dead prompt.** "What do you think?" is dead. Use a specific save/send prompt that names the reason ("save this before your next team meeting"), a real question, or a sharp closing line.

### Hook formulas to pick from (by goal)

| Goal | Formula | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Saves | Number-First Result | A specific number result up front, then "here's the exact thing that changed." |
| Saves | Steal-This Framework (carousel) | Names a repeatable framework, breaks it into parts, ends on a one-slide summary. |
| Saves | Listicle (carousel) | Numbered payoff on slide 1, one item per slide, recap at the end. |
| Shares | Contrarian Truth | Names common advice in the space, states what's actually true, flatly. |
| Shares | Myth-Buster (carousel) | Names a belief her audience holds, corrects it slide by slide. |
| Comments | Relatable Cold-Open | One specific, shared moment, no setup, dropped straight into the feeling. |
| Comments/Follows | Mini-Story Confession | A short real story with an actual cost or surprise, ending on the lesson. |

Pick the formula by what the post needs to earn (per its pillar's funnel job), then fit it to the topic. Don't force every post into the same shape.

### Hashtags

3 to 5 total, sized, not 30. Roughly 2-3 niche (under 50k posts), 1-2 mid (50k-500k), at most 1 broad (500k+). Every tag has to actually match the content. Place at the end of the caption or in the first comment, never mid-caption. Rotate the set per topic, don't reuse the same 5 tags on every post.

## House style (stricter than Instagram's generic default)

- **No em dashes, ever, anywhere.** This project's standing rule overrides the Instagram skill's generic "about 1 per 100 words" allowance. Use commas, periods, colons, or a line break instead.
- Never invent a fact, statistic, quote, or anecdote. Use only what's in the cleared-for-use list above.
- Never name another real company or person without it being pre-cleared.
- Check every date and number against the proof list above before publishing.
- No reveal bridges ("here's the thing," "the result?"), no sincerity announcements ("honestly," "real talk," "not gonna lie"), no engagement bait ("double tap if," "comment YES," "tag 3 friends"), no stacked/hollow rule-of-three.

---

## Broader avoid-list

- The say-do gap in any form: greenwashing, a waste-driven business marketed as sustainable, hustle-culture messaging that glamorizes success at others' expense, or any claim that can't be backed up.
- Fast fashion, in any form, directly contradicts her TEDx thesis.
- Being flattened into "just a stylist."
- No family or marriage content, removed entirely per her direct instruction.
