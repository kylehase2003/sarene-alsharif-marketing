## The one rule that matters most: topic first, evidence second

Never start from "I want to mention the B Corp score" or any other fact. Start from a topic (see the list below), then attach whichever real piece of evidence actually supports it. The same evidence can support different topics over time. This is what keeps the content sustainable instead of repeating the same three achievements over and over.

## Her actual voice

**Vocabulary and rhythm**
- Short, declarative opening sentence that states the thesis immediately, often under 15 words.
- A "but" or "because" sentence used as a pivot partway through.
- Ends on a single compressed, quotable line, often a two-clause parallel structure.
- 6 to 8 hashtags, mixing one topic-specific tag with broader ones.
- Emoji used sparingly, only in warmer/celebratory posts (a milestone, a team moment), never in argument-driven or framework posts.

**Point of view**
- Financial-first, not moral-first. The question is "does a company's stated values actually show up in practice, and what is that gap costing them," not a purely ethical appeal.
- Confident and declarative, not tentative. Do not frame her own realizations as naive discovery ("I thought X, turns out Y," "I used to think... then I realized"). State things as established authority instead. This has been a real, repeated correction on this project, treat it as a hard rule.
- Practitioner, not theorist. She demonstrates the point on herself before asking anyone else to try it.

**What to avoid, confirmed by real rejected drafts on this project:**
- No "I thought X, turns out Y" structure. Reads as inexperienced.
- No identical rhetorical pattern repeated on every line (e.g., every sentence following "it's not X, it's Y"). Reads as mechanical. Vary sentence shape line to line.
- No sincerity announcements ("honestly," "can I tell you"). Hard no.
- No reveal bridges ("here's what," "the result?").
- Not every post needs a personal proof point to close the loop. A framework or opinion piece can stand alone, her credibility comes from the whole body of content, not any single post.
- Don't invent a specific hardship, moment of doubt, or cost if it's not in this document. If a post needs emotional weight, write toward the general true feeling (uncertainty, exposure) without fabricating a specific event.

## Structure: pick what fits, don't force a template

A post is built from one useful point, its source, who's reading it, and the job it needs to do:
- **Top of funnel** (Pillar 1, and Pillar 4's Ask Sarene): make the right reader recognize a problem or perspective. No CTA needed.
- **Middle of funnel** (Pillar 2): build trust in her specifically through lived experience. No CTA needed.
- **Bottom of funnel** (Pillar 1's Business Case content, Pillar 3): make a relevant offer and a clear next step. CTA fits here, if it's a real, available one.

Choose whichever structure suits the material, don't force every post into the same shape:
- A **sequence** for a process.
- A **story** for a real change.
- **Evidence** for a claim.
- A **comparison** for a decision.
