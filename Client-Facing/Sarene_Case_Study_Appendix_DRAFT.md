## The Catalyst

Tad More was already built around caring for people and the planet, helping people keep their clothing longer, and believing the people doing that work deserve respect, fair treatment, and room to grow. But she wanted accountability beyond her own intentions. It's easy for a business owner to say "we do good." She wanted an independent standard that would challenge the business to actually demonstrate it.

She also wanted those values to outlast her, to guide the business as it grows rather than depend on her being present for every decision. And as a small business, she saw a chance to show that responsible practices belong on Main Street, not just at large corporations.

## The Decision

There was a financial cost, but the bigger investment was time and attention. For a small business, that matters, time spent on certification competes directly with serving customers, supporting employees, and running daily operations.

The deeper commitment was being willing to examine whether the business's practices consistently reflected its values. In her own words: "Good intentions are a starting point, accountability requires evidence and a willingness to improve."

## The Audit

An outside, independent audit, not self-graded, scored across five dimensions of how the business actually operates: environment, workers, community, customers, and governance, not just the environmental piece most people assume it checks.
