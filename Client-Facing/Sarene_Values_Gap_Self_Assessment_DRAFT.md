# The Values Gap Self-Assessment

*Draft, reviewed. Blocked on her approval before launch (see `Action_Plan.md` Section 2, Note 14).*

Do your values actually change the way your business operates?

Most organizations can tell you what they value.

The harder question is: where can people actually see those values in the decisions your business makes?

This assessment looks at four places where values become visible: People, Customers, How the Business Is Run, and Environment.

For each question, think about the last 12 months.

---

## 01. People

**1. Has a stated company value changed an actual hiring, promotion, compensation, or termination decision?**
- Yes. I can think of a decision where our values genuinely influenced what we did.
- Sometimes. Our values are part of these decisions, but they don't always shape the outcome.
- Not really. We make these decisions without our values playing much of a role.

**2. In the past year, have you invested in helping an employee develop a skill they weren't yet strong at, rather than expecting them to already have it?**
- Yes. We've made time and effort to help someone grow into their potential.
- We've supported people's development, but it isn't something we do consistently.
- We generally expect people to have the skills they need when they take on a role.

**3. Do everyday working conditions reflect the values your business says it stands for?**
- Yes. The way we work reflects what we say matters to us.
- In some ways. There are parts of our working environment that don't fully reflect our values yet.
- There's a gap between the values we talk about and what working here is actually like.

## 02. Customers

**4. Has a customer situation forced you to choose between protecting your business financially and doing what you believed was right for the customer?**
- Yes. Our values helped guide what we decided to do.
- It's a balancing act. We try to do right by customers, but it doesn't always determine our decisions.
- When the two conflict, the financial needs of the business generally guide our decision.

**5. Have you changed a product, service, policy, or customer experience because it didn't align with a stated company value?**
- Yes. We've changed something because it wasn't living up to what we believe in.
- We've noticed where things don't align, but haven't always followed through with changes.
- We haven't made meaningful changes based on this kind of mismatch.

**6. When a customer isn't the most profitable option, can your team explain how your values should influence how that customer is treated?**
- Yes. Our team understands how our values should guide the way we treat customers.
- It depends on the person or situation. We don't always approach these decisions the same way.
- We haven't clearly established how our values should guide these situations.

## 03. How the Business Is Run

**7. Have you made a business decision that improved profitability, speed, or convenience but came at the expense of your values or your people?**
- Yes. We've faced these trade-offs and actively considered our values when deciding what to do.
- We've considered the trade-offs, but our values don't always influence the final decision.
- When these priorities conflict, our values rarely factor into the decision.

**8. Can employees make decisions based on your company's values without needing a leader's approval every time?**
- Yes. My team understands our values and can use them to make decisions independently.
- It depends. Some decisions are made independently, while others still need my involvement.
- Most decisions still come back to me, even when I'd expect my team to handle them.

**9. If you removed your values from your website and handbook, would someone still recognize them by looking at how your business operates?**
- Yes. Someone could see what we stand for by looking at how we make decisions and run the business.
- Some of our values would be obvious, but others might be difficult to recognize.
- Without telling people what our values are, our actions wouldn't make them very clear.

## 04. Environment

**10. Have you changed an environmental practice or business decision because it better reflected what you believe in?**
- Yes. What we believe about the environment has led to real changes in how we operate.
- We've made some changes, but our environmental values don't consistently shape our actions.
- Our environmental beliefs haven't meaningfully changed how we do business.

**11. Have you changed a purchasing, production, packaging, or operational decision because of its environmental impact?**
- Yes. Environmental impact has influenced specific decisions we've made.
- We consider environmental impact, but it doesn't always affect what we ultimately choose.
- Environmental impact rarely influences these business decisions.

**12. When environmental responsibility conflicts with convenience, speed, or cost, do you have a clear principle for deciding what to do?**
- Yes. We have a clear principle that helps us make these decisions, even when there's a trade-off.
- We know environmental responsibility matters, but our approach depends on the situation.
- We haven't established a clear way to make these decisions when priorities conflict.
